# Notification policy

Telegram is the primary channel. Owner-only local files hold the bot token and chat ID. The default repository configuration disables delivery; the Mac's private production configuration enables it.

From 28 September 2026, a book reaches Telegram only after GPT-6 Sol identifies the actual offered copy as a collector-fit GEM or UNICORN **and** the local bargain screen confirms a substantial price gap. The 175-name tier list, Parr/Badger and Roth 101 guide discovery and context but do not automatically qualify a listing. All 12 source lanes continue scanning broadly. Interesting or uncertain books remain in the local dashboard without a phone alert. Publisher and prize discoveries also remain there.

The bargain screen requires a GBP fixed-price listing, current source evidence, an all-in purchase within the configured cash limit, at least two direct public market pages for the same edition in no better condition, and at least one page showing a sold GBP price and date within three years. It uses the **lowest** checked price as a cautious reference, with the default alert threshold at least 60% below that reference and at least £50 indicative net resale room after a 20% selling-fee allowance, £10 outgoing postage and known incoming postage (or a £20 buffer). The default cash limit is £150 all-in. These values can be changed under `[policy]` in the private `config.toml`. Public listing pages can be incomplete; this is a screening estimate, not a guaranteed resale value or profit. Auctions are kept in the dashboard because the current bid is not a final buy price.

Only `BARGAIN_FIND` events from the current `collector-bargains-v4` review may initiate a book alert. The message gives the all-in estimate, discount to the checked comparable floor, indicative resale room, brief photographer/book context, one opportunity reason and one risk. Buttons link to the seller and sold comparable. The delivery worker rechecks the review, current observation, owner decisions, eBay live price and postage, and source freshness. Old queued `FAST_LEAD`, `RESEARCHED_FIND`, publisher and sweep events are suppressed. Imported historical stock stays silent. Ambiguous Telegram delivery gets at most one clearly marked possible-duplicate retry; provider acceptance is not proof of phone receipt. No purchase, bid or offer is automated.

Replying with a photo or screenshot to an accepted old `RESEARCHED_FIND` or new `BARGAIN_FIND` alert still triggers an image-based Telegram reply. The local scanner has no daily AI research job cap. Lead research, photo replies and wider sweeps run through one research worker; photo replies have priority. Actual Codex account limits still apply. On a provider limit, work stays queued and the worker retries after a temporary backoff. No unresearched book alert is sent. A standalone photo cannot reliably identify its target listing.
